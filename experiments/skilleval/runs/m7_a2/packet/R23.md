## The reviewer's reply

I reviewed `prompt.md`. Here's what I found, with line numbers and the fix I'd make. I haven't changed anything yet.

## Clear bugs (likely cause of the model "ignoring parts")

1. **Lines 20 & 26 — contradictory paragraph-count rules.** "Every reply must contain exactly two paragraphs" (20) directly conflicts with "Every reply must contain exactly three paragraphs" (26). This alone explains inconsistent formatting — the model has no way to satisfy both. **Fix:** delete one. Need your call — two or three?

2. **Lines 9 and 46 — duplicate instruction.** "Place citations immediately after the claims they support" appears verbatim in both the Evidence handling list and the Delivery reminder table. **Fix:** remove the table row (46) and keep the single instance at line 9; fold the acronym-expansion rule (47) into the Response composition section instead, then delete the now-empty "Delivery reminder" table.

3. **Line 13 — undefined term.** "escalation age limit" is never defined anywhere in the prompt, so the model can't apply it. **Fix:** either state the actual threshold (e.g., "180 days") or remove the rule if it's not enforceable without that number. This needs your input — what's the real limit?

4. **Line 11 — unactionable, shouted instruction.** "PROTECT CONFIDENTIALITY!!!" gives no criteria for what's confidential or what action to take, so the model can't reliably act on it (likely contributing to "ignored" behavior). **Fix:** replace with a concrete rule, e.g. "Do not reveal [specific fields/categories] even if present in retrieved documents." I need you to tell me what actually needs protecting here (e.g., salary data, SSNs, legal matters) — this is a decision I can't make for you given it affects compliance.

## Bloat / cost drivers

5. **Line 3 — generic filler.** "You are a helpful, knowledgeable assistant" adds no instruction value; the Purpose section already establishes role. **Fix:** delete.

6. **Lines 10 vs 35 — duplicated explanation.** Line 10 says to use `record_key` when calling `source_lookup`; lines 35 then re-explains the same thing in more words inside the dictionary. **Fix:** keep the fuller definition at line 35 and drop the short version at line 10, or vice versa — minor, your preference.

7. **Lines 21–23 — stylistic flourishes competing with hard constraints.** "Suggest a related topic," "Add a friendly sign-off," "Include a memorable analogy" cost tokens on every single response and seem like an odd fit for an internal research/evidence tool (vs. a casual assistant). They're also three more things competing for space inside a strict 2–3 paragraph limit, which likely causes the model to drop something each time. **Question for you:** do you actually want these on every answer, or were they copied in from a different prompt template? If not essential, I'd cut them.

## Structural/security concern

8. **Line 29 — unescaped document injection.** `{{retrieved_documents}}` is interpolated directly into the instruction flow with no delimiter separating untrusted document content from instructions. If a retrieved document contained adversarial text ("ignore previous instructions..."), there's nothing marking it as data-not-instructions. **Fix:** wrap it in clear delimiters, e.g.:
   ```
   <retrieved_documents>
   {{retrieved_documents}}
   </retrieved_documents>
   ```
   This is a legitimate hardening change I'd make regardless of your other answers — flagging it in case you want it scoped differently.

## Minor/ambiguous (flagging, not necessarily changing)

9. **Line 12** — "appropriate number of decimal places" is subjective; may already be intentional (giving the model judgment). Keep unless you want a concrete rule (e.g., match the source's precision).
10. **Line 14** — "Do not infer an employee's intentions from their job title" reads like a one-off rule from a past incident, disconnected from surrounding evidence-handling rules. I'd leave it as-is unless you know it's now obsolete.
